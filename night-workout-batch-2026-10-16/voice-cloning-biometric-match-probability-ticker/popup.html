Voice Cloning Awareness

Biometric Match Guide · v1.0.0

Educational only — not biometric matching. This guide explains how voice cloning works, its red flags, and consent norms. It performs no audio analysis and matches nothing. Nothing leaves your browser.

How voice cloning works (plain language)

Modern voice-cloning systems learn a person's vocal identity from a short sample — sometimes just seconds of speech. A speaker-encoder extracts a compact "voice fingerprint" (timbre, pitch range, speaking style), and a synthesis model renders arbitrary text in that voice.

Cloning a voice is not the same as verifying a person. Synthesis copies surface acoustics; it does not prove identity, consent, or authenticity.

Consent and misuse

• Cloning someone's voice without their consent — for scams, impersonation, or harassment — can be illegal in many jurisdictions.

• "Hi, it's your grandson, I need money urgently" calls are a classic voice-clone scam pattern: urgency + secrecy + a request to act now.

• Legitimate uses (accessibility narration, dubbing, voice restoration) all involve explicit consent from the speaker.

Verify before you trust

• Call back on a known number, don't reply to the incoming call.

• Agree a family code word for emergencies — a secret phrase only your real family knows.

• Be skeptical of urgent money requests from any unexpected voice message.

Why red flags are only signals

Real recordings can have artifacts too (bad microphones, compression, editing). A flag list raises caution — it never proves a clip is fake, and a clean list never proves it is real.
