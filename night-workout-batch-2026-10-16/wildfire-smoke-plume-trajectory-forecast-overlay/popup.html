    How smoke behaves

    • Travels far. Wildfire smoke can drift hundreds of kilometres — hazy skies far from any fire are common.
    • Sinks at night. Cool evening air traps smoke near the ground; mornings are often the worst.
    • Wind shifts it. A wind change can clear or worsen smoke within an hour — note the wind direction when you log.
    • Fine particles. Smoke is mostly PM2.5, which can travel deep into lungs. Cloth masks do not stop it; N95/P2 respirators help when fitted.

    On smoky days

    • Keep windows and doors closed; run air-conditioning on recirculate if you can.
    • Avoid strenuous outdoor exercise when smoke is heavy.
    • People with asthma, heart conditions, the elderly and young children are most sensitive — check on them.
    • Never rely on this extension for emergency decisions. Follow local fire and health authorities.
