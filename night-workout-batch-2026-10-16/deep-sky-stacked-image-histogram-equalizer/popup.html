Why stack?

Deep-sky images are faint: most signal hides in the darkest 10% of the histogram. Stacking averages many exposures to beat down random noise, then histogram equalization stretches the dark tones so faint nebulosity becomes visible — at the cost of also stretching noise, which is why calibration frames and enough subs matter.
